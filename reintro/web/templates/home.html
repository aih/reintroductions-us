A related bill became law means that this bill did not become law, but another bill of the same Congress did, and either:

  • Congress.gov lists that bill as identical or related to this one, as identified by the House, the Senate or the Congressional Research Service. CRS uses its Text Analysis Program, which built on the bill-similarity algorithms that Ari Hershowitz developed with Daniel Schuman and Charlotte Lee for BillMap;
  • the two bills have the same official title, and three bills or fewer carry it; or
  • the two bills have the same sponsor and either the same official title or the same short title, compared without its year, where three bills or fewer carry that short title.

The bill's page names the law and how it was matched. Some or all of its provisions may already be law, so read the law before reintroducing the bill. The research report describes how Congress.gov identifies related bills and how titles are matched.

How it works

  1. Search for bills by text, number, type, policy area, sponsor, cosponsor or date introduced.
  2. Open a bill to see its draft for the next Congress with the warnings in place.
  3. Check the bills you want, in the results or on a bill's page, and download their drafts as XML, PDF and a warnings list.

The guideline lists what usually changes between Congresses.
